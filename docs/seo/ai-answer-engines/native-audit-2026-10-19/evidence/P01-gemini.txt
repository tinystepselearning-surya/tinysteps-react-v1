Some children struggle with  auditory  blending before they even try print. If you say the separate sounds out loud—like "f-i-sh"—can your child tell you the word is "fish"? If they struggle to blend sounds when heard orally, they will naturally struggle to blend them when seeing letters on a page.

										68 text Working Memory Overload 
Reading a simple three-letter word requires a child's brain to look at a letter, recall its sound, hold that sound in mind while looking at the next letter, recall  its  sound, and so on, until the end of the word. If this process takes too much effort, the beginning of the word is forgotten by the time they reach the end.

										75 text Practice Oral Blending (Sound Games) 
Before asking your child to read words on paper, play blending games without letters. Say sounds slowly and ask them to guess the word.  (Example: "Can you touch your h-a-t?" or "What is this animal? d-o-g".)  This trains the ear and brain to synthesize sounds without the added stress of looking at letters.
