## Purpose

Brick 4 establishes a future-facing reason for strong communication without turning Tiny Steps Speaking into an AI course.

Core proposition:

> When information is easier to generate, children still need to decide what to ask, what to question, how to organise ideas, how to explain their reasoning, how to listen to another person, and how to respond responsibly.

The programme therefore treats questioning, reasoning and communication as transferable human capabilities.

## Tiny Steps communication loop

The page now presents:

**ASK → THINK → ORGANISE → EXPLAIN → LISTEN → RESPOND**

### ASK — Ask useful questions
- clarify the task;
- identify missing information;
- ask follow-up questions;
- avoid accepting the first answer automatically.

### THINK — Think before accepting
- compare ideas;
- notice assumptions;
- decide what needs checking;
- distinguish plausible information from well-supported information.

### ORGANISE — Organise ideas
- sequence information logically;
- prepare a story, explanation, response or argument a listener can follow.

### EXPLAIN — Explain clearly
- use the child's own words;
- use reasons and examples;
- adapt language to the audience.

### LISTEN — Listen and understand
- attend to another viewpoint;
- identify what was actually said;
- ask for clarification.

### RESPOND — Respond thoughtfully
- use new information or feedback;
- adapt to evidence, audience and purpose.

This six-part sequence is a **Tiny Steps teaching framework**. It is not presented as a UNESCO, OECD, UNICEF or WEF framework.

## Important age boundary

Brick 4 explicitly states:

> This does not mean young children need to use AI tools.

The communication habits can be practised through ordinary age-appropriate:
- conversation;
- storytelling;
- explanation;
- discussion;
- questioning.

Brick 4 therefore does not recommend AI use for four-year-olds or prescribe an AI starting age.

## Authoritative context

### UNESCO — AI Competency Framework for Students
https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/ai-competency-framework-students

The UNESCO framework contains 12 competencies across:
- human-centred mindset;
- ethics of AI;
- AI techniques and applications;
- AI system design.

It emphasizes human agency, responsibility and critical judgement of AI solutions.

Tiny Steps uses this as **future-facing education context**, not as a Public Speaking curriculum standard.

### OECD — Learning Compass 2030
https://www.oecd.org/en/data/tools/oecd-learning-compass-2030.html

The OECD Learning Compass:
- emphasizes student agency;
- aims to help students navigate unfamiliar contexts;
- combines knowledge, skills, attitudes and values;
- is globally informed and intended to be locally contextualized.

Tiny Steps uses this to support the broader value of independent thinking and purposeful communication.

### UNICEF Innocenti — Skills for an AI World
https://www.unicef.org/innocenti/reports/skills-ai-world

Published in September 2026, this child-centred work highlights:
- critical thinking;
- information literacy;
- evaluating and verifying information;
- autonomy;
- moderation in AI use;
- human relationships.

Evidence boundary:
- the consultation involved 97 children aged 9–17 across five African countries;
- it is **not** used as evidence about preschool AI use or a universal child-development standard.

### World Economic Forum — Future of Jobs Report 2025
https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/in-full/3-skills-outlook/

Employer survey context identifies:
- analytical thinking as a leading core skill;
- technological literacy;
- empathy and active listening;
- curiosity and lifelong learning;
- creative thinking.

Evidence boundary:
- this is labour-market/employer research;
- it is not a child-development benchmark;
- Tiny Steps does not claim that public-speaking classes guarantee future employment outcomes.

## Product boundary
