## 1. Executive findings and verdict

**Verdict — COMPLETE WITH LIMITATIONS, NOT a search visibility pass.** The live [Speaking & Communication Resource Hub](https://tinystepslearning.com/resources/speaking) is retrievable by the web reader and offers a usable five-cluster educational directory: everyday response, confidence/context, storytelling/organisation, discussion/presentation, and rehearsal/transfer. All **14 unique knowledge destinations** in the frozen speaking-owner registry were individually followed successfully from this hub. Another featured [Why Public Speaking Is Important for Kids](https://tinystepslearning.com/blog/why-public-speaking-is-important-for-kids) route was verified, for **15/15 directly represented speaking knowledge articles** in the two hub discovery layers. The hub source sitemap and blog sitemap include these, but this is source membership, not deployed XML or engine indexing.

Educational boundaries are generally coherent: one-word replies differ from inability to initiate speech; conversation is reciprocal, public speaking is more audience-structured, confidence is observable participation rather than accent/loudness/extroversion, and oral retelling is not memorised theatrical performance. Articles use concrete practices, question stems, story and debate routines, optional video feedback and non-diagnostic language. External oral-language guidance supports interactive communication as an educational approach but does **not** prove Tiny Steps outcomes or formally endorse this programme. <https://educationendowmentfoundation.org.uk/education-evidence/teaching-learning-toolkit/oral-language-interventions>; <https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-english-programmes-of-study>; <https://oracycambridge.org/on-defining-oracy/>.

**Three hub-owned improvements:**
- **A11-F001 P2, directly verified destination/CTA mismatch:** `/resources/speaking` invites families to “Open speaking games” and “Practise speaking” as if ready-to-play digital practice; the linked catalogue currently says **4 listed / 0 playable** and all four “COMING SOON”; the linked specific game is also “COMING SOON” with only instructions. The game catalogue is **A13**, but the hub invitation is **A11**. This is not evidence that a functioning game crashed.
- **A11-F002 P2, navigation/intent handoff:** the hub explores everyday reciprocal speaking and one-word replies but its single “Explore live speaking support” CTA points to `/speaking` (Public Speaking), with **no direct same-level specialist `/spoken-english-classes-for-kids-online` CTA** on the hub. Readers can eventually find Spoken English from articles or Public Speaking, but a direct decision link would reduce mistaken programme choice; don't misstate the existing link as broken.
- **A11-F003 P3, language precision:** hub's “Child gives one-word answers” card under its cluster says **“Diagnose why replies stay short”** while the article explicitly explains it provides an *editorial teaching checkpoint, not a standardised language assessment*. Prefer “Explore why ...” or “Understand why ...”; this is a wording-risk improvement, **not** a claim of actual clinical diagnosis or harm.

**Critical proof gaps:** actual canonical/header/noindex/JSON-LD as delivered, first-party Google/Bing index status, controlled search rank and native AI answer source/citations are **UNKNOWN**. The live XML web retriever's unsupported-content type error and /robots.txt retrieval error are tool limitations, not confirmed site outages. No P0/P1 was reproduced.
