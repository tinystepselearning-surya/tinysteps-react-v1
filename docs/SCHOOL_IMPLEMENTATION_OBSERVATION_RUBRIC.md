# Tiny Steps School Phonics Implementation Observation Rubric

Version: 1.0

## Purpose

This rubric standardises how Tiny Steps Learning Partners review classroom implementation in partner schools. It supports coaching, quality assurance and leadership reporting. It is not a punitive teacher-ranking instrument and should not be used as the sole basis for employment decisions.

The portal review record may remain concise; this rubric defines the evidence behind the reviewer’s implementation judgment.

## Rating scale

- **4 — Secure:** consistently evident, accurate and embedded in the observed lesson.
- **3 — Established:** generally accurate and evident; minor coaching refinements remain.
- **2 — Developing:** partially evident or inconsistent; targeted support is required.
- **1 — Priority support:** absent, inaccurate or materially limiting learner access/progress.
- **N/O — Not observed:** the lesson did not provide a fair opportunity to observe the dimension. N/O must not be converted to zero.

A reviewer should score only what was reasonably observable and record concrete evidence rather than relying on general impressions.

## Dimensions

### 1. Lesson purpose and Tiny Steps routine

**Observe:**

- the teacher knows the intended phonics/reading objective;
- modelling, guided practice, application and checking are purposeful;
- lesson pace allows learners to practise rather than watch passively;
- activities match the taught stage rather than introducing uncontrolled patterns.

**Secure evidence:** the lesson follows the agreed implementation routine naturally while remaining responsive to learner need.

### 2. Phoneme and grapheme modelling accuracy

**Observe:**

- target sounds are articulated accurately and consistently;
- unnecessary schwa is avoided where it would interfere with blending;
- grapheme–phoneme correspondences match the agreed teaching sequence;
- the teacher distinguishes sound from letter name when relevant;
- valid accent variation is respected and does not become false correction.

**Priority-support trigger:** repeated inaccurate modelling that is likely to teach an incorrect sound/spelling relationship.

### 3. Blending and decoding practice

**Observe:**

- learners are prompted to use taught sound knowledge rather than picture/first-letter guessing;
- the teacher models blending clearly;
- children receive enough independent attempts;
- unfamiliar controlled words are used where appropriate to demonstrate genuine decoding;
- support is reduced as independence develops.

**Secure evidence:** learners increasingly solve controlled words through the taught code with appropriate independence.

### 4. Segmenting, encoding and spelling practice

**Observe:**

- learners identify sounds in spoken words;
- the teacher supports left-to-right phoneme/grapheme mapping;
- spelling practice uses patterns the children have been taught;
- feedback identifies the relevant sound/spelling relationship rather than simply supplying the whole answer.

**Secure evidence:** learners regularly move from spoken word to represented sounds with increasing independence.

### 5. Checking for understanding and learner participation

**Observe:**

- the teacher samples responses across the group rather than relying only on confident children;
- individual misconceptions are noticed;
- children receive enough opportunities to say, read, build or write the target material;
- the teacher adapts support when learners are not secure;
- participation remains age-appropriate and psychologically safe.

**Secure evidence:** the teacher can state what learners understood because the lesson contains observable checks, not only whole-class repetition.

### 6. Feedback, correction and pacing

**Observe:**

- errors are corrected promptly and respectfully;
- correction returns the learner to the relevant sound/process;
- excessive prompting does not mask whether the child can decode independently;
- pacing balances repetition with cognitive load;
- successful responses receive specific reinforcement without creating answer dependence.

**Secure evidence:** feedback improves the learner’s strategy and independence, not merely the immediate answer.

### 7. Decodable/controlled text alignment

**Observe when connected text is used:**

- the text predominantly reflects the phonics knowledge learners have been taught;
- untaught patterns are limited and intentionally supported;
- the teacher encourages decoding before guessing from context/pictures;
- comprehension discussion follows access to the text rather than replacing word reading.

Use **N/O** when the observed lesson did not include connected text.

### 8. Inclusion, multilingual/EAL and access responsiveness

**Observe:**

- task instructions are understandable to learners at their English-language stage;
- the teacher distinguishes English-language knowledge from decoding knowledge where possible;
- home languages and accent variation are treated respectfully;
- reasonable school-agreed access arrangements are followed;
- support removes barriers without supplying the scored/target reading response.

**Secure evidence:** all participating learners can access the intended reading practice without lowering or changing the taught phonics objective unnecessarily.

## Observation summary

At the end of the visit, the reviewer records:

1. **Implementation strengths** — 1–3 evidence-based points.
2. **Priority coaching action** — the single highest-value next step where possible.
3. **Learner evidence noticed** — for example independent blending, persistent guessing, sound-confusion pattern, or increased automaticity.
4. **Recommended support** — modelling, co-teaching, micro-training, resource adjustment, assessment check, or follow-up observation.
5. **Next review date/window.**

## Mapping to portal implementation rating

The portal’s high-level rating is a management summary, not a mathematical teacher score.

### Strong

Use when the critical observed dimensions are consistently established/secure, no material accuracy issue is present, and learner practice reflects the agreed methodology.

### Developing

Use when implementation is broadly underway but one or more important dimensions remain inconsistent and require coaching.

### Needs support

Use when a material implementation barrier is likely to affect learner outcomes—for example inaccurate sound modelling, insufficient learner decoding practice, persistent guessing strategies, or a failure to apply the agreed methodology.

Do not automatically map an arithmetic average to these labels. A critical accuracy problem may require “Needs support” even if other dimensions are strong.

## Moderation

- New Learning Partners should co-observe or compare ratings on sample lessons before independently issuing formal implementation judgments.
- Periodically double-observe a sample across Learning Partners to check interpretation consistency.
- Discuss meaningful rating disagreement and refine examples/guidance.
- Avoid comparing teachers as a league table; use the rubric to improve implementation quality and identify support needs.
- Keep this rubric version linked to the review period so changes in criteria remain traceable.
