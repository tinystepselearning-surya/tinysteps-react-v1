## Publishing rule going forward

Use the week number only for the internal editorial calendar. **Do not publish new URLs as `week-47-*`, `week-48-*`, etc.** Each article should use an evergreen parent-search-intent title and slug so it can remain indexable and useful beyond one calendar week.

Every new article should:

1. Solve one clear parent problem or answer one high-intent question.
2. Give a quick answer near the top for AEO/GEO.
3. Include examples, a practical home routine, common mistakes, and FAQs.
4. Link to the most relevant existing evergreen blog, parent guide, course page, and assessment/demo path without forcing commercial copy.
5. Avoid duplicating an existing canonical article. If an older noindex weekly article already covers the idea, use it as source material and create one stronger evergreen canonical page rather than another weekly archive page.
6. Use the real publication date; do not backdate articles simply to fill historical weeks.

## Why these topics were selected

### Phonics and reading gaps

The site already has strong material on basic phonics, blending, CVC words, long vowels, r-controlled vowels, phonics classes, reading confidence, and the August 2026 CBSE/systematic-phonics authority cluster. The next useful parent layer is **application and progression**: choosing decodable reading material, knowing what comes after CVC words, decoding multisyllabic words, and understanding why spelling remains developmental after phonics.

### Grammar gaps

The old weekly archive covers tenses, conjunctions, subject–verb agreement and creative writing, while the newer evergreen layer already discusses sentence formation and children who know grammar but still make mistakes. The new plan therefore targets **high-frequency applied grammar gaps** parents see in real speech and writing: articles, prepositions, sentence boundaries and punctuation.

### Public speaking and communication gaps

Current content already addresses confidence, one-word answers, children who understand English but do not speak, and general spoken-English confidence. The new plan goes deeper into **observable communication behaviours**: conversation turn-taking, WH-question answers, show-and-tell structure, voice modulation, storytelling/retelling and impromptu speaking.

### Commercial intent without becoming sales copy

`one-to-one-vs-group-english-classes-for-kids` is intentionally included as one decision-stage article. It should compare formats fairly and explain which learners benefit from each format before describing Tiny Steps' one-to-one model.

## Evidence themes to preserve while writing

Use evidence-aligned principles when drafting the articles:

- Early reading requires sound awareness, sound–letter connections, decoding and regular connected-text reading.
- Fluency is not speed alone; accuracy and expression support comprehension.
- Children benefit from explicit, cumulative practice rather than isolated worksheets.
- Strong communication includes complete sentences, story retelling, question answering, topic maintenance and conversational turn-taking.
- For spoken English practice, parents should allow thinking time, avoid interrupting every error, and correct selectively after the child finishes speaking.
