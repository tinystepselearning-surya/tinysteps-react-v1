# Tiny Steps Early Reading Benchmark — Operational Protocol

Version: TSERB-1.0

## Purpose

The Tiny Steps Early Reading Benchmark (TSERB) is an **internal instructional and programme-monitoring framework** for partner schools. It is designed to answer a practical management question: are children demonstrating stronger early-reading skills as the Tiny Steps phonics implementation progresses?

TSERB is not presented as a nationally or internationally standardized, norm-referenced, diagnostic, clinical, admissions, or high-stakes assessment. The TS-0…TS-9 levels are Tiny Steps instructional descriptors. They may be used alongside a school’s own national, IB, Cambridge, bilingual, or other curriculum and assessment framework, but they do not replace that framework.

## International-use principles

1. **Curriculum-compatible, not curriculum-replacing.** Tiny Steps measures the foundational reading skills addressed by the agreed implementation plan. Partner schools retain their own wider curriculum, reporting, and accreditation framework.
2. **Progress from starting points matters.** Management should see baseline, later evidence, coverage, and matched growth rather than a single attainment number in isolation.
3. **Language context matters.** A child learning English as an additional language must not be interpreted as having a reading-comprehension deficit solely because of unfamiliar vocabulary or oral-language demands that are outside the construct being assessed.
4. **Home languages are assets.** TSERB assesses English foundational reading where that is the programme goal; it does not rate the value, development, or proficiency of a child’s home language.
5. **Fair access.** Reasonable access arrangements may be used when they remove an unrelated barrier without coaching or changing the reading skill being measured. Material accommodations should be documented consistently.
6. **Accent and pronunciation fairness.** A valid pronunciation or accent variation should not be scored as a decoding error when the child has represented the intended phoneme/grapheme relationship accurately for the assessment purpose.
7. **Low-stakes use.** TSERB results are for teaching, intervention, implementation review, and management improvement. They must not be used by Tiny Steps as the sole basis for admissions, exclusion, retention, diagnosis, or punitive teacher ranking.
8. **Whole-child boundary.** TSERB is a focused early-reading framework. It is not a whole-child early-years battery and does not claim to measure social-emotional development, physical development, mathematics, general cognition, or complete oral-language proficiency.

## Design principles

1. **Low teacher burden.** Assessment happens at defined checkpoints rather than after every lesson.
2. **No child login requirement.** Children are assessed by an adult using controlled assessment materials.
3. **Aggregate school reporting.** The school portal stores section-level summaries in this phase, not individual child profiles.
4. **Decoding over memorisation.** Where developmentally appropriate, assessment forms should include unfamiliar controlled words and a small set of pronounceable pseudowords aligned to already-taught graphemes.
5. **Equivalent forms.** Baseline, checkpoint, mid-programme, and final forms should use equivalent difficulty without repeating the exact same word list.
6. **Instructional alignment.** Children should never be penalised for phonics patterns that have not yet been taught in the programme being evaluated.
7. **Evidence coverage is visible.** Reports retain the number assessed and the section-count snapshot so management can see assessment coverage.
8. **No single metric decides quality.** TS level, domain evidence, teacher training, curriculum stage, and classroom review should be interpreted together.
9. **Versioned administration.** Assessment form, scoring guide, administration instructions, and assessment version must remain traceable so results can be interpreted correctly later.
10. **Equivalent conditions.** Where growth is compared, administration conditions should be materially comparable across checkpoints or any meaningful difference should be recorded.

## Recommended checkpoint cadence

- **Baseline:** before or at the start of implementation.
- **Checkpoint 1:** approximately 6–8 instructional weeks after baseline.
- **Mid-programme:** around the middle of the agreed implementation period.
- **Final:** near the end of the programme/academic-year implementation window.
- **Custom:** only when a school needs an additional intervention/recovery check.

Tiny Steps may adjust checkpoint timing to the school calendar. International partner schools may operate January–December, March–February, August–July, or other academic calendars; the implementation plan should record the school’s actual calendar and use comparable instructional intervals rather than assuming an Indian academic year. The portal intentionally does not require daily or weekly student scoring.

## Core domains

### 1. Phonological awareness

Assess orally without requiring print where appropriate. Examples include rhyme recognition, initial/final sound awareness, oral blending, and oral segmentation. The exact task set should match the child’s instructional stage.

### 2. Sound knowledge

Check accurate recall of the grapheme–phoneme correspondences that have already been taught. Use a mixed order rather than curriculum sequence.

### 3. Blending and decoding

Use controlled unfamiliar words aligned to taught phonics. For children ready for it, include a small pseudoword sample to distinguish decoding from memorised sight vocabulary.

### 4. Segmenting and spelling

Say controlled words aloud and ask children to represent the sounds using letters/graphemes already taught. Score the intended phoneme/grapheme evidence consistently.

### 5. Connected-text reading

Only use when the learner has reached an appropriate decoding stage. Use instructionally appropriate controlled sentences or decodable passages aligned to taught phonics. Observe accuracy, automaticity, phrasing, and reliance on guessing.

### 6. Comprehension

Comprehension should be assessed only on text the learner can independently access at the intended instructional level. Avoid unnecessary cultural or vocabulary loading. Do not interpret decoding difficulty, limited English vocabulary, or unfamiliar background knowledge as the same construct as comprehension without additional evidence.

## TS-0…TS-9 instructional levels

- **TS-0 — Pre-reading:** developing listening, rhyme, oral blending, and phonological awareness; independent print decoding is not yet demonstrated.
- **TS-1 — Sound awareness:** recognises a growing set of explicitly taught letter–sound correspondences.
- **TS-2 — Supported blending:** blends simple VC/CVC words with modelling, prompts, or physical blending support.
- **TS-3 — Independent CVC:** independently decodes unfamiliar regular CVC words using taught sounds.
- **TS-4 — Digraph decoding:** decodes words containing taught common digraphs and consolidates simple controlled sentence reading.
- **TS-5 — Long vowels and patterns:** applies taught long-vowel and common vowel-pattern knowledge to unfamiliar words.
- **TS-6 — Complex decoding:** decodes longer words, blends, and increasingly complex taught phonics patterns.
- **TS-7 — Sentence reader:** reads instructionally aligned controlled sentences with improving accuracy, phrasing, and automaticity.
- **TS-8 — Passage reader:** reads instructionally appropriate controlled or decodable passages aligned to taught phonics with developing fluency and literal comprehension.
- **TS-9 — Confident early reader:** applies taught phonics independently across connected text with established early-reading fluency and comprehension at the assessed instructional level.

A TS level is a programme descriptor, not an age-equivalent score. A younger child at a higher TS level or an older child at a lower TS level should not be described as being a certain number of months or years ahead/behind on the basis of TSERB alone.

## Administration workflow for a 150–200 child school cohort

### Full checkpoint

1. Tiny Steps supplies the relevant equivalent-form assessment pack, administration guide, and scoring sheet.
2. School/Tiny Steps agree a short assessment window and identify any documented access arrangements or English-language considerations that may affect administration.
3. Children are assessed individually or through carefully designed small-group tasks depending on the domain.
4. The assessor marks item-level performance on the paper/digital assessment sheet; teachers are not required to maintain a daily app dashboard.
5. Tiny Steps/Learning Partner checks scoring consistency and consolidates section results.
6. Only the aggregate section summary is entered into the School Partnership Portal in the current phase.
7. Management receives coverage, TS distribution, domain evidence, baseline-to-later growth where matched checkpoint evidence exists, and clear interpretation notes.

### Between full checkpoints

A Learning Partner may conduct a **small audit sample** (for example, approximately five children from a section) using unseen controlled items to verify that classroom implementation is translating into decoding behaviour. Audit samples should inform coaching; they should not be mixed into full-cohort benchmark growth unless they meet the same administration and sampling rules.

## Multilingual, EAL and accessibility administration

- Give task instructions in clear, familiar language and use unscored practice examples where needed so that children understand the task format.
- Do not explain or rehearse the answer to scored items.
- Where oral vocabulary is not the target construct, avoid making unfamiliar vocabulary the reason a child fails a phonics/decoding item.
- Accept valid accent/pronunciation variants when they preserve the phonemic relationship being assessed.
- For children with documented access needs, reasonable adjustments may include additional processing time, appropriate visual size/spacing, seating/environment adjustments, or an alternative response mode where the target construct is unchanged.
- Do not convert an accommodated result into a directly comparable claim if the accommodation materially changed the construct or task difficulty.
- If a material access arrangement or language adaptation was used for a cohort, record it in the assessment notes and apply it consistently at comparable checkpoints wherever appropriate.
- Do not diagnose dyslexia, speech/language disorder, intellectual disability, or another condition from TSERB results. Refer concerns through the school’s own qualified support pathway.

## Scoring integrity

- Use a written scoring guide for each assessment form.
- Keep administration prompts consistent.
- Do not teach the exact test words immediately before assessment.
- Do not reuse the same form at every checkpoint.
- Record absent/non-assessed children separately from children who score at TS-0.
- Never force the level distribution to equal enrolment if some children were absent; the portal records assessed count and coverage separately.
- A TS level should reflect the highest level at which the learner demonstrates the defined critical skills with the required independence, not a simple average of unrelated tasks.
- Section average TS level in the portal is derived from the entered TS-level distribution; it is a communication summary, not a substitute for domain evidence.
- Do not compare section or school averages without showing the assessed cohort size and coverage.
- Do not treat a difference between two sections as a teacher-effect estimate; cohort composition, attendance, language background, starting point, implementation time, and other factors may differ.

## Moderation and quality assurance

For initial school pilots, Tiny Steps should use an explicit moderation process:

1. Calibrate assessors against the same scoring guide before live assessment.
2. Double-score a random sample of completed assessments, especially during early implementations or after an assessment-form revision.
3. Record disagreements, resolve the scoring rule, and update assessor guidance where ambiguity is found.
4. Keep assessment forms and scoring guides under version control; never silently change an existing form while retaining the same version identifier.
5. Review whether baseline and later forms are genuinely comparable in content coverage and difficulty before interpreting growth.
6. Flag low assessment coverage or materially different administration conditions in management interpretation.
7. Periodically review items for cultural, linguistic, accessibility, and construct-irrelevant barriers across the school contexts in which Tiny Steps operates.

Tiny Steps should establish empirical moderation targets only after sufficient real administration data exists rather than inventing a reliability threshold in advance.

## Validation boundary

Before describing TSERB as a **validated, standardized, norm-referenced, internationally normed, age-standardized, or diagnostic assessment**, Tiny Steps would need a separate psychometric validation programme. That work would normally include evidence for intended score interpretations, reliability/measurement consistency, fairness, administration consistency, item/form equivalence, appropriate samples across relevant learner populations, and external expert review.

Until that work is completed, the defensible product language is **“Tiny Steps internal Early Reading Benchmark”**, **“internationally informed programme benchmark”**, or **“school implementation and early-reading progress framework.”**

## Management interpretation

Management reporting should emphasize:

- assessment coverage,
- shift in TS-level distribution,
- matched baseline-to-later growth,
- domain-level changes where both checkpoints assessed the same domain,
- sections needing implementation support,
- teacher-training completion,
- classroom-review evidence and recommended action,
- relevant assessment limitations or accommodations.

A leadership report should distinguish **attainment**, **growth**, **implementation quality**, and **data quality**. It should not collapse them into one opaque score.

The objective is to demonstrate implementation quality and visible early-reading progress without turning school teachers into data-entry operators.
