# Tiny Steps Early Reading Benchmark — Operational Protocol

Version: TSERB-1.0

## Purpose

The Tiny Steps Early Reading Benchmark (TSERB) is an **internal instructional and programme-monitoring framework** for partner schools. It is designed to answer a practical management question: are children demonstrating stronger early-reading skills as the Tiny Steps phonics implementation progresses?

TSERB is not presented as a nationally standardized, norm-referenced, or diagnostic clinical assessment. The TS-0…TS-9 levels are Tiny Steps instructional descriptors.

## Design principles

1. **Low teacher burden.** Assessment happens at defined checkpoints rather than after every lesson.
2. **No child login requirement.** Children are assessed by an adult using controlled assessment materials.
3. **Aggregate school reporting.** The school portal stores section-level summaries in this phase, not individual child profiles.
4. **Decoding over memorisation.** Where developmentally appropriate, assessment forms should include unfamiliar controlled words and a small set of pronounceable pseudowords aligned to already-taught graphemes.
5. **Equivalent forms.** Baseline, checkpoint, mid-programme, and final forms should use equivalent difficulty without repeating the exact same word list.
6. **Instructional alignment.** Children should never be penalised for phonics patterns that have not yet been taught in the programme being evaluated.
7. **Evidence coverage is visible.** Reports retain the number assessed and the section-count snapshot so management can see assessment coverage.
8. **No single metric decides quality.** TS level, domain evidence, teacher training, curriculum stage, and classroom review should be interpreted together.

## Recommended checkpoint cadence

- **Baseline:** before or at the start of implementation.
- **Checkpoint 1:** approximately 6–8 instructional weeks after baseline.
- **Mid-programme:** around the middle of the agreed implementation period.
- **Final:** near the end of the programme/academic-year implementation window.
- **Custom:** only when a school needs an additional intervention/recovery check.

Tiny Steps may adjust checkpoint timing to the school calendar. The portal intentionally does not require daily or weekly student scoring.

## Core domains

### 1. Phonological awareness

Assess orally without requiring print where appropriate. Examples include rhyme recognition, initial/final sound awareness, oral blending, and oral segmentation. The exact task set should match the child’s instructional stage.

### 2. Sound knowledge

Check accurate recall of the grapheme–phoneme correspondences that have already been taught. Use a mixed order rather than curriculum sequence.

### 3. Blending and decoding

Use controlled unfamiliar words aligned to taught phonics. For children ready for it, include a small pseudoword sample to distinguish decoding from memorised sight vocabulary.

### 4. Segmenting and spelling

Say controlled words aloud and ask children to represent the sounds using letters/graphemes already taught. Score the intended phoneme/grapheme evidence consistently.

### 5. Connected-text reading

Only use when the learner has reached an appropriate decoding stage. Use controlled sentences or passages aligned to taught phonics. Observe accuracy, automaticity, phrasing, and reliance on guessing.

### 6. Comprehension

Comprehension should be assessed only on text the learner can access at the intended reading level. Do not interpret decoding difficulty as a comprehension deficit.

## TS-0…TS-9 instructional levels

- **TS-0 — Pre-reading:** developing listening, rhyme, oral blending, and phonological awareness; independent print decoding is not yet expected.
- **TS-1 — Sound awareness:** recognises a growing set of explicitly taught letter–sound correspondences.
- **TS-2 — Supported blending:** blends simple VC/CVC words with modelling, prompts, or physical blending support.
- **TS-3 — Independent CVC:** independently decodes unfamiliar regular CVC words using taught sounds.
- **TS-4 — Digraph decoding:** decodes words containing taught common digraphs and consolidates simple sentence reading.
- **TS-5 — Long vowels and patterns:** applies taught long-vowel and common vowel-pattern knowledge to unfamiliar words.
- **TS-6 — Complex decoding:** decodes longer words, blends, and increasingly complex taught phonics patterns.
- **TS-7 — Sentence reader:** reads controlled sentences with improving accuracy, phrasing, and automaticity.
- **TS-8 — Passage reader:** reads age-appropriate decodable passages with developing fluency and literal comprehension.
- **TS-9 — Confident early reader:** applies phonics independently across connected text with fluent early reading and comprehension.

## Administration workflow for a 150–200 child school cohort

### Full checkpoint

1. Tiny Steps supplies the relevant equivalent-form assessment pack and scoring sheet.
2. School/Tiny Steps agree a short assessment window.
3. Children are assessed individually or through carefully designed small-group tasks depending on the domain.
4. The assessor marks item-level performance on the paper/digital assessment sheet; teachers are not required to maintain a daily app dashboard.
5. Tiny Steps/Learning Partner checks scoring consistency and consolidates section results.
6. Only the aggregate section summary is entered into the School Partnership Portal in the current phase.
7. Management receives coverage, TS distribution, domain evidence, and baseline-to-later growth where matched checkpoint evidence exists.

### Between full checkpoints

A Learning Partner may conduct a **small audit sample** (for example, approximately five children from a section) using unseen controlled items to verify that classroom implementation is translating into decoding behaviour. Audit samples should inform coaching; they should not be mixed into full-cohort benchmark growth unless they meet the same administration and sampling rules.

## Scoring integrity

- Use a written scoring guide for each assessment form.
- Keep administration prompts consistent.
- Do not teach the exact test words immediately before assessment.
- Do not reuse the same form at every checkpoint.
- Record absent/non-assessed children separately from children who score at TS-0.
- Never force the level distribution to equal enrolment if some children were absent; the portal records assessed count and coverage separately.
- A TS level should reflect the highest level at which the learner demonstrates the defined critical skills with the required independence, not a simple average of unrelated tasks.
- Section average TS level in the portal is derived from the entered TS-level distribution; it is a communication summary, not a substitute for domain evidence.

## Recommended quality assurance

For initial school pilots, Tiny Steps should double-score a sample of assessments across assessors and review disagreements. Before describing TSERB as a validated or standardized assessment, Tiny Steps would need a separate psychometric validation programme covering reliability, validity, administration consistency, item/form equivalence, and an appropriate reference/norm sample.

## Management interpretation

Management reporting should emphasize:

- assessment coverage,
- shift in TS-level distribution,
- matched baseline-to-later growth,
- domain-level changes where both checkpoints assessed the same domain,
- sections needing implementation support,
- teacher-training completion,
- classroom-review evidence and recommended action.

The objective is to demonstrate implementation quality and visible early-reading progress without turning school teachers into data-entry operators.
