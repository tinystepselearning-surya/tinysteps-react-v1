Week 8 Learning Spotlight

Tricky Words & Dreamy Speeches

This week we focused on sight words that need extra practice, conjunctions that glue ideas together, and a My Dream Place speech packed with vivid details.

Phonics Focus

Master sight words: the, was, said, come, do, want, you, they

Grammar Skill

Use and, but, because to connect ideas

Speaking Goal

Plan and deliver a “My Dream Place” speech with sensory details

Phonics

Tricky Words on Repeat

Learners built sight word towers, traced the words in sand, and played fast-paced bingo to practise instant recognition. We focused on memorising the shape and sequence of each tricky word.

  • Review five sight words a day with flashcards or sticky notes around the house.
  • Play “word detective”—circle tricky words in bedtime stories.
  • Celebrate progress with a quick cheer or sticker chart.

Sight word sprint! the, was, said, come, do, want, you, they — see if your reader can spot them in tonight’s story.

Grammar

Conjunctions Connect Ideas

We combined simple sentences using and, but, and because. Learners acted out mini scenarios and chose the best connector to show addition, contrast, or reason.

  • Ask which conjunction fits while reading: “Should we join these ideas with and, but, or because?”
  • Build silly sentences together by rolling a die to choose the conjunction.
  • Create a visual anchor chart that shows when to use each connector.

We’re joining ideas with and, but, and because. Try asking “Why?” to spark that because sentence at home.

#GrammarForKids #Conjunctions #SentenceBuilding #TinyStepsLearning

Public Speaking

My Dream Place Speech

Students planned speeches with four prompts: Where is it? What can I see and hear? What will I do there? Who comes with me? We practised expressive voices and confident body language.

  • Help your child sketch their dream place and label sensory details.
  • Practise the speech in short bursts—introduction today, details tomorrow.
  • Invite a family audience or record a video postcard describing the dream place.

Dream place planners! Ask: Where is it? What do you see, hear, and do? Who comes along? Finish with how you feel there.

Support at Home in 15 Joyful Minutes

Short, consistent practice turns tricky words and speeches into confident habits. Try one of these ideas.

Sight Word Treasure Hunt

Hide sight words around the house. Each time your child finds one, they read it and stick it on a chart.

  • Use a timer to make it an exciting challenge.
  • Swap roles so your child hides the words for you next.
  • Keep the chart visible for quick daily review.

Conjunction Spinner

Create a simple spinner with and, but, because. Spin, then complete a sentence together.

  • Use sentence starters like “I love the park…”
  • Encourage your child to explain why they chose the conjunction.
  • Record the best sentences in a mini booklet.

Dream Place Stage

Set up a cosy corner as a stage. Add a map or drawing of the dream place and practise presenting for 2 minutes.

  • Change the audience—family members, toys, or a video call.
  • Offer specific praise: “I loved how you described the colours!”
  • End with a reflective question: “What detail will you add next time?”

Progress You Can Celebrate

Watch for your child spotting sight words instantly, choosing conjunctions thoughtfully, and speaking about dream destinations with sparkle. Share these wins with your Relationship Manager so we can plan the next stretch goals together.
